## The persona

**Alex.** A homeowner. Owns a home battery, an EV charger, and an air conditioner — three
different devices, bought from three different manufacturers at three different times. He has
never heard of a DERControl event and he never needs to.

---

## Part A — Alex's journey, six beats

This is the full technical sequence, already finalized (built and verified in the kWh investor
reference room). It compresses into six narrative beats — the same count and cadence as the
attached reference — one continuous event: a utility calls for demand flexibility, Alex's three
devices answer automatically, and he gets paid for it.

**Beat 1 — He connects three devices, once.**
*Caption: "Three devices, one gateway."*
Alex's battery speaks RS-485. His EV charger speaks Zigbee. His air conditioner only has a
vendor cloud app. None of that changes. A kWh gateway finds all three, registers them, and
issues each one a certificate. From here on, every one of them speaks the same language:
IEEE 2030.5.

**Beat 2 — His utility can now see him.**
*Caption: "His utility sees exactly what he has."*
The aggregator and the utility can now see what capacity Alex has, where it sits on the grid,
and what it's allowed to do. The utility commissions a demand flexibility program on his
feeder — this evening, a two-hour window, a price, an obligation.

**Beat 3 — He's enrolled without doing anything.**
*Caption: "He's enrolled automatically."*
Alex's battery and charger are enrolled into the program. When the window opens, one
instruction goes out from the utility — and the gateway translates it into whatever each
device actually understands underneath.

**Beat 4 — His devices respond. He doesn't.**
*Caption: "The battery discharges. He doesn't touch a thing."*
This is the beat the reference video nails and the one to hold longest: the battery discharges,
the charger pauses, the air conditioner eases back a couple of degrees. Alex is not asked to do
anything. He might not even notice.

**Beat 5 — One number, not two.**
*Caption: "One verified number. Both sides trust it."*
Every device is measured against its own baseline. The same verified number goes to the
aggregator and to the utility — nobody is settling against two different readings of what
happened.

**Beat 6 — He gets paid.**
*Caption: "Alex gets paid for what he delivered."*
Not for signing up. For what his devices actually did. kWh never holds or moves the money — it
measures and verifies, and the aggregator pays him directly.

---

## Part B — zoom out: this is one example, on a network

After beat 6, pull back. What Alex just lived through — one event, one feeder, one customer —
is the smallest unit of something much bigger.

**Beat 7 — Scale.**
*Caption: "This wasn't one house. It's thousands."*
Widen from Alex's single event to a view of many customers, many feeders, the same
infrastructure running the same loop simultaneously. Demand flexibility, the thing Alex just
did, is one service running on top of it.

**Beat 8 — More services, same assets.**
*Caption: "The same assets. More services."*
Name, don't just imply, the services beyond demand flexibility that run on the same
underlying gateway infrastructure: **asset management**, **asset securitization and
tokenization**, and **energy trading**. These are not hypothetical add-ons bolted onto a
different product — they're additional services running on exactly the same connected assets
Alex already has.

**Beat 9 — The network effect, stated plainly.**
*Caption: "More gigawatts. More services. More value per asset."*
Close on the actual growth mechanic, visualized as a climb, not just narrated: as the number
of gigawatts under management grows, more services stack onto that same base of assets, and
the value per asset rises as a result. This is the flywheel — it is not three separate wins,
it's one compounding one.

**Closing line.**
*"Every energy asset, made visible and dispatchable."* — kWh Electric's own line, already in
use on the product site. Use it verbatim as the closing card, not a rewritten version of it.

## Notes for whoever is directing this

- Alex is one consistent persona throughout Part A. Don't introduce a second named character.
- Beat 4 is the emotional center of Part A — the "he doesn't have to do anything" moment is
  the entire value proposition in one beat. Give it real weight, don't rush past it into beat 5.
- Part B's three named future services (asset management, securitization/tokenization, energy
  trading) are deliberately not detailed further here — they're a widening gesture, not a
  second product demo. Keep them to a beat each, not a full explanation.
- If timing forces a cut, cut from Part B's services list before cutting Part A. Alex's journey
  is the whole point; the "more services" beats are the widening, not the substance.
